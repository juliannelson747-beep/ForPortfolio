Abstract

This case study examines existing sediment-related conditions, salmon passage, and river connectivity along the Lower Snake River. GIS and Sentinel-2 imagery are used to map relative turbidity in Lower Granite Reservoir and compare its upper, middle, and lower reaches. Available agency monitoring records provide context for interpreting satellite patterns and describing broader river conditions. The study establishes a baseline for discussing potential dam-removal effects using published research.

Research Question

How can sattelite imagery, GIS, and agency monitoring records be combined to characterize existing turbidity patterns and provide context for almon passage and river conditions along the Lower Snake River?

Data & Methods

Data Sources

  • Copernicus Sentinel-2 imagery: multispectral bands used to calculate water and relative turbidity indices.
  • USGS monitoring records: available turbidity, sediment, and streamflow observations used to support interpretation.
  • USACE and other agency records: salmon passage, dam locations, and river-condition information.
  • USGS elevation data and supporting GIS layers: terrain, basin boundaries, and river features used for study-area mapping.
